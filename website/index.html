How it works

A short lesson each day, read aloud by one warm voice. You pick 2, 5, or 10 minutes.

1

Pick an animal friend

Twelve round, soft plush friends: a dog, a cat, a fox, a bear, a bunny, an owl, a frog, a duck, a pig, a penguin, a lion, and a koala. Your child picks one, and a favorite theme or three: dinosaurs, trucks, space, animals, bugs, the ocean, or castles. Their animal sits in the nest on the home screen and stars in every story.

2

One small lesson a day

A letter and its sound, “m, as in moon.” Then your child slides their animal along a track and hears the sounds join: “m… a… t… mat!” A finger trace with numbered strokes, a short story, and a color, shape, or number moment finish the day. On Friday the week's letters come back for a gentle review.

3

Words grow on a ladder

One letter, then two, then short words, then a tiny story they can sound out themselves. Later come sh, ee, and the magic e in cake. Stars are for trying, never for being right.

26 weeks, in reading order

Two sounds a week, in the order children can start reading with them, not A to Z. By week 2 they can sound out mat; by week 4, sand.

Weeks 1 to 14 are the single letters. Weeks 15 to 26 are the sounds two letters make: sh and ch, vowel teams like ee and ai, the magic e in cake and kite, and ar, or, er, ir. The two-minute Where to start check, or a teacher's lesson place, can start a child at any week.

What's inside

Reading leads the day. Explore adds numbers, colors, time and money, building, science, and coding, with a gentle start for ages 3 to 5 and harder work waiting for ages 5 to 7.

Words Letter sounds, sound it out, tracing letters and their name, phonics, and a reader each week
Numbers Count, hear and trace numbers, fit and trace shapes, more or less, and add small groups
Colors Hear a color and tap it, mix two paints, and paint their animal
Time & Money Day and night, my day, a friendly clock, coins, a pretend shop, three jars, and a lemonade stand
Build A bridge, a tower, ramps, and simple machines
Science Grow a plant, animal homes, the body, weather, the senses, and sink or float
Coding Say hello, take me home, what comes next, first then, if then, make a dance, and read the code

Games sit in the dock on every child's home screen: Hatch the Egg, Letter Pop, Feed the Animal, Rhyme Match, Memory Flip, and Spin & Say. Coding starts with no reading at all: arrows move their animal, and picture blocks make it hop, sing, or grow a flower. Ages 5 to 7 add a repeat, fix a bug, and see a short line of code on each block.

Questions

Is it a subscription?

No. The first two weeks of reading and the first activity of each Explore area are free for good. One payment opens the rest, for every child in your family.

What ages is it for?

Ages 3 to 7. Reading starts with letter sounds and a gentle pace for ages 3 to 5. Ages 5 to 7 go on to phonics, longer stories, half hours, making change, and code blocks. A grown-up can adjust the pace.

Does it need an account or the internet?

Neither. There is no sign-in and nothing to type. Everything, including every recording of the narrator's voice, lives on the device, so the app works on a plane or at grandma's house without Wi-Fi.

Whose voice reads the lessons?

One narrator, recorded line by line: every letter sound, every word, every story page, and even the letters of your child's name as they trace it. The app never hands a line to the phone's robot voice.

Which animals can my child pick?

A dog, a cat, a fox, a bear, a bunny, an owl, a frog, a duck, a pig, a penguin, a lion, or a koala. Their animal is their profile picture, the hero of their stories, the slider they drag across a word, and the one wearing the hat they earned.

Is there a book?

Yes. LittleNest Readers, Book 1: First Sounds is a $9.99 paperback workbook for ages 3 to 5 that follows the app's first four weeks, with the same letter order and the same stroke order. The QR code inside opens littlenestlearning.app/book1, with free bonus stories. The app costs the same with or without the book; there is no code to type.

What about my child's data?

A profile is a first name or initial, an age range, and an animal from the app. It stays on your device. There are no ads, no tracking, and no analytics. Read the privacy policy.

Can a teacher see my child's progress?

Only if you share it. The Progress page shows a short code with lessons finished, practice days, and how many sounds your child knows. Give it to the teacher and they type it in. There's no name in it, and nothing is sent on its own.

Can more than one child use it?

Yes. Add a profile for each child. Each one keeps their own animal, stars, and place in the lessons, and the unlock covers all of them.

Got a new phone or tablet?

Open the grown-up menu, choose Full LittleNest, and tap Restore purchase. With Family Sharing turned on in your Apple account, your family's other devices get it too.

Not right for your family?

Apple handles App Store refunds. Ask at reportaproblem.apple.com. If a refund is given, the app goes back to the free weeks.

Is there an Android version?

Not yet. LittleNest is for iPhone and iPad first. Ask us and we'll let you know when that changes.
